# Attendance

## 1. Purpose

The employee keeps a personal account of attendance and paid time off. The account stays separate from the company's
figures. The employee compares the account with those figures.

The account is calculated entirely from the employee's own record: the working calendar, the facts recorded below, and
the paid-time entitlement. The company's reported minutes and balance sit beside the account for comparison. The
company's reported minutes and balance never change the account's own figures.

A recorded absence is time the employee treats as actually taken. It is not a request still waiting on the company.

## 2. Working time

| Item              | Rule                            |
|-------------------|---------------------------------|
| Ordinary workdays | Monday through Friday           |
| Morning           | 08:00 up to 12:00 (240 minutes) |
| Lunch             | 12:00 up to 13:30, not work     |
| Afternoon         | 13:30 up to 17:30 (240 minutes) |
| Full day          | 480 minutes                     |
| Clock             | The company's local time        |

Only minutes inside scheduled work can be missed. Lunch, a weekend, and a holiday do not consume paid time off.

Scheduled work on a full day is the morning interval and the afternoon interval. A shift is one of those intervals,
either the morning or the afternoon.

The employee may mark a whole day as not scheduled. The employee may instead mark only one shift as not scheduled. The
employee may also mark a day that would otherwise be off as scheduled work. The employee may instead mark only one
shift of that day as scheduled work. A date outside employment has no scheduled work.

## 3. Facts in the account

### 3.1 Arrival

The employee records one arrival time on a date. Seconds are ignored, so 09:00:59 is 09:00. Seconds stay dropped until
the company decides otherwise.

Arrival shows when presence starts. It does not show an early departure or a temporary exit during the day.

### 3.2 Paid absence

| Kind              | What is recorded             | Missed scheduled time                                                                    |
|-------------------|------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Late coverage     | The time late coverage ends  | From the start of scheduled work until that time                                         |
| Early departure   | The time the employee left   | From that time through the end of scheduled work                                         |
| Temporary absence | A start and an end           | The recorded interval                                                                    |
| Half day          | Morning or afternoon         | That shift, 240 minutes                                                                  |
| Full day          | A range of dates             | 480 minutes on each scheduled day in the range, and none on a day with no scheduled work |

A recorded paid absence counts only the minutes that overlap scheduled work.

### 3.3 Remote work

Remote work is a full day or one shift. It is not an arbitrary clock range.

Remote work counts as time worked. It costs no paid time off. No arrival is required during remote work.

### 3.4 Adjustments

An adjustment is a signed number of minutes on a date, with a reason. It adds or removes paid time for a cause other
than that day's work.

The adjustment changes the balance at the start of that date, before any absence on that date is paid.

## 4. Missed minutes

For one day:

1. Take that day's scheduled work.
2. Take the minutes of each recorded paid absence that overlap scheduled work.
3. Take uncovered absence, using the rules that follow this list.
4. Combine the recorded paid absences and the uncovered absence so each scheduled minute is counted once.
5. The length of that combination is the missed minutes.

These rules decide which scheduled minutes are uncovered absence:

- Remote work is not uncovered absence. A recorded paid absence is not uncovered absence. The employee is not expected
  to be present during remote work or during a recorded paid absence.
- When an arrival is recorded, presence starts at the arrival. The employee is still expected before the arrival.
  Scheduled minutes before the arrival are uncovered. Scheduled minutes after the arrival are not uncovered.
- When no arrival is recorded, every scheduled minute during which the employee is still expected is uncovered.
- Lunch is not uncovered absence, because lunch is not scheduled work.

A recorded paid absence remains missed when the employee arrives during that absence. A recorded paid absence also
remains missed when its minutes are marked as remote work. Remote work does not cancel a recorded paid absence.
When remote work and a recorded paid absence overlap, the overlapping minutes count once, as recorded paid absence.
The day shows that overlap.

Missed minutes cannot exceed that day's scheduled minutes. Scheduled minutes on a day cannot exceed 480.

Late coverage until 09:00 and an arrival at 09:05 come to 65 missed minutes. The five minutes after 09:00 are added
once. They are not added on top of a separate 60-minute charge.

## 5. Paid and unpaid time

Missed minutes are paid from one balance, in work-date order.

At the start of a day, the available balance is the balance that earlier workdays left, after any adjustment that
takes effect on this day is applied. The paid minutes are the lesser of the day's missed minutes and that available
balance. The unpaid minutes are the missed minutes that remain. The balance after the day is the available balance minus the
paid minutes. The balance after the day is never below zero.

A later day's paid and unpaid split follows from the balance that earlier days left behind. An absence does not reduce
the balance before its own date.

Unused minutes carry into later years. They do not expire. The balance is not reset at the start of a year.

## 6. Entitlement

A calendar year throughout which the employee is employed grants an entitlement of 14 days. Those 14 days are 6,720
minutes. The year grants the entitlement once.

The entitlement for a partial year is not yet settled. Until the company settles that entitlement, a partial year
grants the nearest whole minute of:

6,720 × employed calendar days in that year ÷ calendar days in that year.

Employed calendar days in a year run from the later of the employment start and 1 January, through the earlier of the
employment end and 31 December. Both ends count. When employment has not ended, the formula uses 31 December as the
employment end. A year of employment from 1 January through 31 December grants 6,720 minutes.

## 7. What each day shows

Each day with scheduled work, and each day on which an adjustment takes effect, shows enough to compare with the
company:

- the scheduled intervals
- the arrival time, when one is recorded
- the remote work
- the recorded paid-absence intervals
- the uncovered absence
- any overlap between a recorded paid absence and remote work
- the missed minutes
- the paid minutes
- the unpaid minutes
- the balance after that day

## 8. Examples

| Situation                                                | Missed minutes | Paid time                                             |
|----------------------------------------------------------|---------------:|-------------------------------------------------------|
| Arrives at 08:00, no absence                             |              0 | None                                                  |
| Arrives at 09:00, no absence                             |             60 | 60, when the balance covers them                      |
| Late coverage until 09:00, arrives at 09:05              |             65 | 65, counted once                                      |
| Morning off, arrives at 13:30                            |            240 | 240                                                   |
| Morning off, arrives at 14:00                            |            270 | 270, counted once                                     |
| Absent 10:00–11:00, otherwise present from 08:00         |             60 | 60                                                    |
| Leaves at 16:30                                          |             60 | 60                                                    |
| Remote work for the full day, no arrival                 |              0 | None                                                  |
| No arrival, no remote work, and no recorded paid absence |            480 | As much as the balance can pay. The rest is unpaid.   |
| Full-day recorded paid absence on a scheduled day        |            480 | 480, once                                             |
| Recorded paid absence on a day with no scheduled work    |              0 | None                                                  |
| 150 minutes missed, 90 minutes of balance left           |            150 | 90 paid and 60 unpaid                                 |
| Morning recorded paid absence, afternoon remote work     |            240 | 240. No arrival is required during that remote work.  |

## 9. Where the accounts can disagree

This account can still disagree with the company at these points:

- The company has not settled the basis of a partial year: calendar days, workdays, or months. Until the company
  settles the basis, the account uses the calendar-day formula in section 6.
- Arrival seconds are dropped, as in section 3.1.
- Remote work is a full day or one shift.
- Paid time is taken on the workday itself. A future absence leaves the balance on an earlier day unchanged.
